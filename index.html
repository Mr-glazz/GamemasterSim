EXISTING SYSTEM PRESERVED — DEVELOPMENT MOVES FORWARD

0. Core Development Directive

PRESERVE THE WORK. Existing working systems, UI, layouts, mechanics, player systems, economy, blueprints and match systems are not to be unnecessarily replaced or rebuilt.
  • Preserve existing UI.
  • Preserve existing navigation.
  • Preserve existing player cards.
  • Preserve existing roster systems.
  • Preserve existing economy.
  • Preserve existing blueprint rules.
  • Activate existing code that is not yet connected.
  • Fix inconsistencies with the blueprint.
  • Only add new systems when they genuinely improve an existing area.

This is refinement and activation, not a restart.

1. Player Database

  • Core attributes use a non-arcade 1–20 scale.
  • Jump Shot (JS)
  • Dribbling (DR)
  • Stealing (ST)
  • Perimeter / Outside Defense (OD)
  • Match IQ

Physical Modifiers

  • Height
  • Weight
  • Speed

Physical characteristics operate independently of the flat skill ratings. Weight can provide meaningful low-post leverage.

Player skills do not simply disappear because a player rests or is injured.

3. College Player Rules

  • 18–22: primary college-player sweet spot.
  • 17: extremely rare.
  • College players do not automatically receive prohibited special skills.
  • College players follow the established rarity system.
  • High skills remain rare.
  • Scouting value can change according to performance and form.

4. Match IQ

IQ is one of the hardest things to earn in the game.

Match IQ can influence:

  • Decision making
  • Passing reads
  • Defensive positioning
  • Offensive decisions
  • Tactical execution
  • Pressure situations
  • Blueprint interactions
  • Situational awareness

IQ is earned through legitimate long-term development and game experience. It is not handed out casually.

5. Team Rating

Team Rating is a real simulation factor, not merely a number displayed on the standings page.

It interacts with:

  • Player ability
  • Match IQ
  • Blueprint
  • Tactical matchup
  • Form
  • Health
  • Fatigue
  • Chemistry
  • Traits
  • RNG
  • Opponent strength

6. Professional Match System

Professional games use a 48-minute clock.

The established 100-possession backend concept remains the foundation of the professional match engine.

Each possession evaluates player attributes, physical modifiers, IQ, blueprint, tactical scheme, matchup, team factors and controlled variance.

7. RNG / Double-Dice Variance

Dice 1 — Basketball Variance

A 1–20 variance check introduces realistic basketball unpredictability. Lower-tier players can occasionally beat elite defenders.

Dice 2 — Mood / Character Strain

Traits may create additional pressure effects:

  • Erratic
  • Arrogant
  • Nervous
  • Gritty Underdog
RNG creates uncertainty. RNG does not replace the basketball model.

An elite player can have a bad game, but the outcome must remain consistent with the blueprint's underlying factors.

10. Match Finality & Replay

Once a game is completed, its outcome is final.
  • Final score is saved.
  • Player performances are saved.
  • Box score is saved.
  • Team statistics are saved.
  • Standings effects are saved.
  • Financial effects are saved.

A completed game can be watched again through a replay. Replay uses the saved game and does not reroll the outcome.

11. League-Wide Living Simulation

When one team plays, the league plays.

Teams play at slightly different scheduled times while the week advances.

  • Games occur.
  • Training occurs.
  • Players develop.
  • Form changes.
  • Injuries and recovery occur.
  • Statistics update.
  • Standings update.
  • Finances update.
  • Markets move.
  • Youth systems progress.
  • Scouting progresses where scheduled.

The league should feel alive even when the manager is not directly watching every event.

14. Player Management

Release

Releasing a player sends the player into Free Agency according to the established blueprint rules.

Fire / End Career

Fire means the player retires.

Release and Fire are different actions.

15. Rookie Academy

  • Every franchise has a Rookie Academy.
  • Rookies can be generated and trained.
  • Rookies can play Academy games.
  • Rookie games use two 12-minute halves.
  • Rookies accumulate statistics.
  • Rookies can develop before the draft.
  • Rookies enter the Rookie Draft.
  • Teams select rookies.
  • Selected rookies join their franchise.
  • They then enter the normal player-development ecosystem.
Rookie talent is not artificially capped. Rare Diamond and Red Diamond rookies remain possible according to the established rarity system.

32. Development Rules

DO

  • Preserve existing working code.
  • Reuse existing functions.
  • Connect systems rather than duplicate them.
  • Keep the existing UI.
  • Respect the blueprint.
  • Make surgical changes.
  • Test changes incrementally.
  • Keep placeholder/test players where useful.
  • Generalize examples only when necessary.

DO NOT

  • Do not unnecessarily rebuild the UI.
  • Do not replace functioning systems.
  • Do not remove established mechanics.
  • Do not create duplicate economic systems.
  • Do not create a competing match engine.
  • Do not introduce constant mid-game tactic switching.
  • Do not allow RNG to determine everything.
  • Do not make elite players automatically win.
  • Do not randomly make weak players dominate without logical factors.
  • Do not change blueprint rules without approval.
